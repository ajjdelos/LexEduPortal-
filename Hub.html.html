Art. 1156

1. General Provisions on Obligations

Legal Definition (Art. 1156, Civil Code)

"An obligation is a juridical necessity to give, to do or not to do."

Juridical Necessity: In case of non-compliance, courts of justice may be called upon to enforce performance or recover economic damages.

Four Essential Requisites of an Obligation

1. Active Subject (Creditor)

The One Who Has The Right

The party entitled to demand the fulfillment of the obligation.

2. Passive Subject (Debtor)

The One Bound to Perform

The party from whom the performance or prestation is legally demandable.

3. Prestation / Object

The Particular Conduct Required

The act required to be observed (to give, to do, or not to do).

4. Efficient Cause (Vinculum Juris)

The Juridical Tie

The legal relation binding the parties (e.g., law, contract, tort).

Art. 1157

2. Five Primary Sources of Obligations

1. Law (Ex Lege)

Imposed directly by statute. Cannot be presumed (e.g., duty to pay income tax under National Internal Revenue Code).

2. Contracts (Ex Contractu)

Arises from meeting of minds between parties. Has force of law between contracting parties (Art. 1159).

3. Quasi-Contracts (Ex Quasi-Contractu)

Lawful, voluntary, and unilateral acts based on equity to prevent unjust enrichment (e.g., Solutio Indebiti, Negotiorum Gestio).

4. Delicts / Crimes (Ex Maleficio)

Civil obligations arising from criminal offenses (restitution, reparation of damage, indemnification).

5. Quasi-Delicts / Torts (Ex Quasi-Delicto)

Acts or omissions causing damage to another through fault or negligence where no pre-existing contractual relation exists (Art. 2176).

Art. 1179+

3. Primary Kinds of Obligations

Pure Obligation

Not subject to any condition or period. Demandable at once.

Conditional Obligation

Fulfillment depends on a future and uncertain event (Suspensive vs. Resolutory).

Obligation with a Period

Performance depends upon the arrival of a day certain (must necessarily come).

Joint Obligation

Each debtor is liable only for his proportionate share of the debt.

Solidary Obligation

Each debtor is liable for the entire performance of the obligation.

With Penal Clause

Contains an accessory undertaking to guarantee performance and substitute damages.

Art. 1231

4. Extinction of Obligations

1. Payment / Performance Complete delivery of money or rendering of service.
2. Loss of Specific Thing Thing perishes without fault of debtor prior to default.
3. Condonation / Remission Gratuitous forgiveness of debt by creditor.
4. Confusion / Merger Qualities of creditor and debtor unite in same person.
5. Compensation Two persons become mutual creditors and debtors of each other.
6. Novation Substitution or modification of obligation by a new valid one.
Art. 1305+

5. Essential Requisites of Contracts

Definition of Contract (Art. 1305)

"A contract is a meeting of minds between two persons whereby one binds himself, with respect to the other, to give something or to render some service."

1. Consent

Concurrence of offer and acceptance. Must be intelligent, free, and voluntary (Art. 1319).

2. Object Certain

The subject matter of the contract. Must be within commerce of men and licit (Art. 1347).

3. Cause / Consideration

The essential reason why parties enter into the contract (Art. 1350).

Art. 1380+

6. Four Classes of Defective Contracts

1. Rescissible Contracts

Valid until rescinded

Validly agreed upon, but causes economic lesion or damage to one of the parties or a third person (e.g., contracts in fraud of creditors).

2. Voidable Contracts

Valid until annulled

Defective due to incapacity of one party or vitiated consent (mistake, violence, intimidation, fraud).

3. Unenforceable Contracts

Cannot be sued upon

Entered into without authority, or fails to comply with the Statute of Frauds (Art. 1403).

4. Void or Inexistent Contracts

No effect whatsoever

Lacks essential elements, or whose cause/object is contrary to law, morals, or public policy. Cannot be ratified.
